# Lagrange v1.2.0: cables, heat in the parts, a robot's own interference (release notes)

v1.2 is the second feature release on the stable 1.x line. It adds three kinds of physics that a
robot does to itself or drags behind it: **tethers** that sag, snag and tangle, **heat inside
every part** worked out from its material, and the **magnetic field of its own wiring**. Each comes
with a viewer, tests against closed forms or published references, an example and a tutorial.
Multicopters fly more like real ones, and the documentation site has a new front page.

Everything frozen by RFC 0007 changed only by addition. The new schemas are `TetherAPI_1`,
`ThermalMaterialAPI_1` and `HeatSourceAPI_1`. `EmiSourceAPI_1` gained a `wire` type and three
attributes. The protocol gained `FieldVolume.prim_path`. All of these are checked by the
frozen-surface tests, and v1.0 and v1.1 files, plugins, profiles and recordings still load.

**Status:** released on 2026-11-11 as `v1.2.0`, on the owner's request. Documentation:
<https://open-physical-systems.github.io/Lagrange/>.

## Highlights

### Tethers and cables (RFC 0011)

- **`TetherAPI_1`** (or `--tether X,Y,Z,LINK[,LENGTH]`) ties a cable from an anchor to a body.
  The `tether` domain solves it by small-step XPBD at 1 ms.
  - The cable sags and drags in the wind.
  - It touches every collider with friction, catches on edges, tangles on itself and pushes and
    pulls the bodies it meets.
  - A winch can hold its length, hold a tension, or run free.
- **Checked against** the catenary, a taut string and the capstan equation.
- **The viewer** draws every cable.
- Docs: docs/physics/tether.md.

### Heat inside every part (RFC 0012)

- **Materials and heat sources** come straight from a URDF: `<thermal_material preset="fr4" ...>` and
  `<heat_source power= efficiency= xyz= size=>`, or the same in an overlay or USD. There are 14
  presets, each with its source.
- **The thermal domain's `conduction` fidelity** cuts each link into cells. It works out:
  - conduction, orthotropic where a material needs it;
  - contact conductance to the parent link;
  - natural and forced convection;
  - radiation in the share of the surroundings each face sees.
  A motor's losses heat the link that holds its stator.
- **Checked against** the lumped limit, the fin with a convecting tip, the semi-infinite solid
  and a two-block contact, from 0.01 % to 2 %. On a sample LED heat sink it is also compared with
  OpenFOAM: on a 2.5-million-cell mesh, OpenFOAM's sink sits where Lagrange's correlations put
  it. How close the LED's own hot spot comes is still open (docs/physics/heat-conduction.md).
- **The viewer's Thermal shading** colours every part cell by cell, and the inspector shows each
  link's mean, max and min.
- **Thermal sensors:**
  - The sample plugin `lagrange_thermal_probe` adds a contact thermistor and an infrared spot
    thermometer.
  - Thermal cameras read each part with its material's emissivity.
- **New commands:**
  - `lagrange heat ROBOT --ambient-temperature K` gives the steady temperatures in seconds.
  - `lagrange foam-cht ROBOT --ambient K [--radiation]` writes and runs the same robot as an
    OpenFOAM conjugate heat transfer case, then reads its temperatures back.

### A robot's own interference (RFC 0013)

- **Wire sources.** `EmiSourceAPI_1` gains `wire` sources, the exact Biot–Savart sum over a
  route with its return conductor, driven by any current signal.
- **Motor leads.** `--emi-motor-leads SPACING[,X,Y,Z]` lays a lead from the battery to each motor
  carrying its bus current.
- **What the compass reads.** Every magnetometer reports `heading_error` and `interference`, the
  ratio ArduPilot's compassmot reports. The sample quadcopter's 13.7 A in hover puts its heading
  0.09° off, 1.1 % of the Earth's field.
- **The viewer** slices |B| upright through the robot on a log scale. Its Overlays panel lists
  the effects on compasses, CAN buses and radio links under **Coupled into**.

### Multicopters

- **Full throttle** under PX4 and ArduPilot is each rotor's loaded top speed, from its motor and
  battery.
- **`--thrust-limit FRACTION`** caps every rotor at a share of its full thrust.
- **The built-in controller:**
  - ramps its velocity like PX4;
  - keeps its set point on a leash, so a ceiling neither flips nor holds a drone;
  - takes the surface under it as the ground, so it can fly off a bridge or a roof.
- **Motor constants.** The samples' motors take KV against the bus voltage,
  Ke = 60/(2π·KV·√3).

### Viewer

- **Reset** restarts a live run.
- **Record** (or Ctrl+R) records a live run on the server.
- **New drawing:** tethers, the heat in each part, and the |B| field around the robot.

### Examples, tutorials, site

- **New examples:** `lagrange_example_heat` and `lagrange_example_compass`.
- **New tutorial:** docs/guide/tutorial-heat-and-emi.md, run headless by
  `tools/ci/tutorial_heat_and_emi.sh`.
- **New example profile:** `profiles/examples/led_heatsink.yaml`.
- **The documentation site's front page:** a gallery, the domains and a quick start.

## Upgrading from 1.1

Nothing to migrate: files, plugins, profiles and recordings of 1.0 and 1.1 load as they are.
The new behaviour is opt-in:
- a link's thermal material (with the site's ambient temperature);
- `--tether` or `TetherAPI_1`;
- `--emi-motor-leads` or `wire` sources;
- `--thrust-limit`.

Two behaviours change for every run:
- **Full throttle under PX4 and ArduPilot.** It is each rotor's loaded top speed, where it used
  to be 850 rad/s. The sample's PX4 airframe and ArduPilot parameters were re-tuned to match.
- **The samples' motor constants.** They use the √3 above, so the sample quadcopters spin
  slightly faster for the same voltage.
