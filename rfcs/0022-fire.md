## Summary

Fires that start, grow, spread to nearby fuel, heat what is near them, make smoke that blinds
cameras and lidars, and can be put out the way real fires are: by water that cools the fuel, by
wind that blows a small flame off, or by smothering (foam over the fuel, or too little oxygen).
The model is the engineering correlations fire protection engineers use: a t² or pool-fire heat
release rate, Heskestad's flame height and plume, a point-source radiation model, Evans' sprinkler
suppression decay, an oxygen balance for enclosed spaces, and soot yield with a specific
extinction coefficient for smoke. Each is checked against its closed form, and against a
published value where one was found. A robot fights the fire with a nozzle (RFC 0020) whose jet
lands where physics puts it and pushes back on the robot. CFD of flames is out of scope. NIST's
Fire Dynamics Simulator (FDS) is offered offline, files only, as OpenFOAM is for air. Every
change is an addition under RFC 0007.

## Motivation

The owner asked for fire that water, wind and smothering can put out, "following real-world
physics". Firefighting and fire-inspection robots are an active field: ground robots with
monitors, drones with hoses or extinguisher payloads, and inspection robots in tunnels and
plants. The software they run needs a world where:

- smoke takes away the lidar and the camera, and the thermal camera still sees;
- radiant heat raises the robot's own temperatures, so its motors derate and its electronics
  overheat on the existing thermal paths;
- a stream of water applied at too low a rate, or on a burning liquid, does not put the fire out,
  and one applied well does, at the speed real fires go out;
- a drone's downwash fans a fire as well as blowing a small one out.

## Non-goals

- Computational fluid dynamics of flames, combustion chemistry, flame spread over surfaces by
  pyrolysis, flashover, backdraft and explosions. FDS does these offline (§10).
- Fire protection engineering. Like the EMI and radiation domains, the numbers are for testing
  robot software, not for designing buildings or proving safety. The documentation says so.
- Wildland fire spread (see "Also worth adding").
- Chemical inhibition by dry powder and clean agents (no correlation of the same quality was
  found for this draft; open question 10).
- Burns to people. Tenability is reported as an incident heat flux on each walking human, not
  as an injury.

## Design

the burning area's footprint and whose pose places it. It moves with its body: a burning cart
carries its fire.

#### 1.1 Presets

Presets are a table in `modules/fire/data/fuels.yaml`, one row per fuel, each value with its
source. Proposed first rows (every number TODO(verify) from the cited source before step 2; no
number is in this draft on purpose, so there was nothing to check yet): wood crib, wood pallet stack, polyurethane foam furniture,
cardboard boxes, heptane pool, gasoline pool, diesel pool, and a car (a published HRR curve for a
passenger car). Sources: the SFPE Handbook of Fire Protection Engineering (5th ed., Springer 2016)
chapters by Babrauskas (burning rates) and Tewarson (yields), and Babrauskas, "Estimating large
pool fire burning rates", *Fire Technology* 19 (1983). The licence for copying a table of values
from a handbook is checked before they ship (facts are not copyrightable, but the owner decides,
open question 3).

### 2. The heat release rate

**Growth (`t_squared`).** From ignition at `t_i`: `Q(t) = α·(t − t_i)²`, capped at
`Q_max = q''·A_burning`. The standard growth classes are slow, medium, fast and ultrafast, which
reach 1055 kW (1000 Btu/s) in 600, 300, 150 and 75 s. So
`α = 1055 kW / t_g²` = 0.00293, 0.01172, 0.0469, 0.1876 kW/s². Slow, medium and fast are checked:
Evans and Stroup, NBSIR 85-3167 (1985), the DETACT-T2 listing, sets α = 2.9306, 11.722 and
46.889 W/s² for them (1000 Btu/s at 600, 300 and 150 s). Ultrafast (75 s) is not in that report,
and NFPA 72's annex is not openly readable: TODO(verify) the annex's current number and its
ultrafast row. The four
names are allowed as tokens for `fire:alpha` in the overlay forms.

**Pool fires (`pool`).** `Q = ṁ''∞·(1 − e^(−kβ·D))·ΔH_c·A`, D the pool's equivalent diameter
(Babrauskas 1983).

**Burn-out.** The burnt mass is `∫Q dt / ΔH_c`. When less than 20 % of `fuelMass` remains, Q is
scaled by the remaining share over that 20 %, a linear decay. That is a modelling choice to
document, not a measured law (open question 5).

**Burning area.** `A_burning = A·(1 − φ_foam)` (§5.3).

### 3. Flame, plume and radiation

- **Flame height** (Heskestad): `L = 0.235·Q^(2/5) − 1.02·D` (L and D in m, Q in kW).
- **Virtual origin** (Heskestad): `z0 = 0.083·Q^(2/5) − 1.02·D`.
- **Plume centreline velocity** above the flame (Heskestad):
  `u0 = 3.4·(g/(c_p·T∞·ρ∞))^(1/3)·Q_c^(1/3)·(z − z0)^(−1/3)`, with `Q_c = (1 − χ_r)·Q`. T∞, ρ∞ are
  `env/air_temperature` and `env/air_density` when the weather runs, else the site's.
- **Tilt in wind** (the AGA correlation as given by Mudan and Croce in the SFPE Handbook):
  `u* = u_w / (g·ṁ''·D/ρ_a)^(1/3)`, `cos θ = 1` for `u* ≤ 1`, `cos θ = u*^(−1/2)` above. `u_w` is
  the wind at the flame, `env/wind/velocity` plus the rotors' wake (§5.2).
- **Radiation to targets** (point-source model): `q'' = χ_r·Q·cos β / (4π·R²)`. The source sits at
  half the flame height along the tilted axis, R is the distance to the target, and β is the angle
  between the target's surface normal and the line to the source. It is only valid away from the
  flame. McGrattan, Baum and Hamins (NISTIR 6546, 2000, §3) call it "accurate in the far-field, but
... overly conservative within a few fire diameters"; the module warns closer than that.
TODO(verify) a numeric R/D limit: Beyler's chapter (below) was not openly readable. An
  occlusion ray from the source to each target removes blocked flux.

The equations are the SFPE Handbook's forms (5th ed., Springer 2016: chapter 13, Heskestad,
"Fire Plumes, Flame Height, and Air Entrainment", pp. 396–428, and chapter 66, Beyler, "Fire
Hazard Calculations for Large, Open Hydrocarbon Fires", pp. 2591–2663; numbers, titles and pages
from the chapters' Crossref records, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4939-2565-0_13 and _66). The chapters'
text was not openly readable; the FDS Validation Guide (6.11.1, eq. 3.38) quotes the flame
height in its dimensionless form `L = D·(3.7·Q*^(2/5) − 1.02)`.
Q is in kW in these correlations; the module works in W and converts, with the constants named
and their units commented (§8 of CLAUDE.md).

**Heat into the robot.** For a lumped `ThermalAPI_1` node, the absorbed power is
`ε·q''·A_rad/4`. Here ε is `thermal:emissivity` (a grey body absorbs as it emits), and `A_rad/4`
is the mean projected area of a convex body of surface `A_rad` over all directions (Cauchy's
theorem). The normal is then averaged out, so no `cos β`. It is written as
`fire_radiation/<node>/loss_power`, which the thermal network already adds to the node named
`<node>` (its `<class>/<node>/loss_power` rule). So the thermal module does not change. For the
conduction fidelity (RFC 0012), the flux on each outer cell face with its own `cos β` is phase 2.
Robots near a fire then heat, derate (`thermal/<n>/derating`, existing) and trip their
over-temperature limits, through code that exists.

**People.** Each walking human (RFC 0009) gets `human/<n>/heat_flux` (W/m², on a vertical
cylinder) so a planner that keeps people away from a fire can be checked against a tenability
threshold the user chooses (ISO 13571 gives one; TODO(verify) its value and clause: iso.org
refused the request and the standard is not openly readable; Purser and McAllister's SFPE
chapter 63, "Assessment of Hazards to Occupants from Smoke, Toxic Gases, and Heat", is the other
place to read it).

### 4. Smoke

- **Production.** Soot mass rate `ṁ_s = y_s·Q/ΔH_c`.
- **Transport.** The soot is emitted as Lagrangian puffs every step (100 ms). Each puff is a
  Gaussian of mass `ṁ_s·dt` that rises at the plume's centreline velocity at its height and is
  carried by the wind and the rotors' wake at its centre. Its radius grows as the plume's
  (`b ∝ (z − z0)`, Heskestad's plume width; TODO(verify) the coefficient and the temperature
  factor: in SFPE chapter 13, not openly readable). A puff stops rising under a ceiling (a ray up meets a collider) and spreads under it.
  It leaves the domain beyond the region's box. There is no deposition. Deterministic: fixed
  order, no random numbers (invariant 7). This is a far simpler model than CFD. It gets the
  smoke in roughly the right place and quantity, and the documentation says how far to trust it
  (open question 6).
- **Optics.** The extinction coefficient is `σ = K_m·ρ_soot`, with the specific extinction
  coefficient `K_m = 8700 m²/kg` for flame-generated smoke at visible wavelengths (Mulholland and
  Croarkin, "Specific extinction coefficient of flame generated smoke", *Fire and Materials* 24,
  2000, pp. 227–230). The FDS User's and Technical Reference Guides (6.11.1) state it as
  "8700 m²/kg ± 1100 m²/kg at a wavelength of 633 nm" for most flaming fuels, citing that paper.
  For the lidar bands (905, 1550 nm) and the thermal camera's band, σ is scaled by `(λ_vis/λ)^a`
  (soot absorption). Zhu, Choi, Mulholland et al. (*Proc. Combust. Inst.* 29, 2002) measured the
  dimensionless extinction constant `K_e = K_m·ρ_s·λ` of soot at 543.5 to 1565 nm and found it
  roughly constant (means 7.95 to 10.0), which makes `a ≈ 1` up to 1565 nm (abstract read only).
  TODO(verify) the exponent in the thermal band (8–14 µm), where no measurement was found. With any `a` near 1, LWIR extinction is an order of
  magnitude below visible, which is why thermal cameras see through smoke.
- **Into the sensors.** The fire module rasterises the puffs at 2 Hz into an in-memory NanoVDB
  scalar field, `env/smoke/extinction` (Field3D, 1/m, visible). This needs a
  `fields::scalarField` beside the existing in-memory `fields::vectorField`. The cameras and
  lidars already integrate one optical-depth field per ray (`fieldDepth`, the fog field). They
  read the smoke field too and add the two depths, scaled per band. Lidar returns from inside
  smoke reuse the existing fog clutter model. The `visibility_sensor` kind reads the local σ and
  reports `S = C/σ`, with C = 3 for light-reflecting and C = 8 for light-emitting signs (the FDS
  User's Guide 6.11.1, eq. 22.23 and `VISIBILITY_FACTOR`, citing Mulholland, "Smoke Production
  and Properties", SFPE Handbook 3rd ed., 2002; the relation is Jin's, whose own papers were not
  opened).

### 5. Putting it out

#### 5.1 Water

- **Application.** Water reaches a fire as an application rate `w''` (kg/(m²·s), numerically
  mm/s of water) averaged over its burning area. It comes from nozzles (§8), from sprinklers
  placed in the scene (a nozzle fixed to the world with a spray pattern), or from rain
  (`env/rain_rate`).
- **Suppression (class A).** Evans' model (D. D. Evans, "Sprinkler fire suppression algorithm for
  HAZARD", NISTIR 5254, 1993): once water above the critical rate reaches the fuel, the heat
  release rate decays as `Q(t) = Q(t_a)·exp(−(t − t_a)/τ)` with `τ = 3.0·w''^(−1.85)` s (w'' in
  mm/s). That gives τ = 410 s at 0.07 mm/s, the value the report states for 0.61 m wood cribs,
  as read in the report's full text (abstract and eq. 3: `τ = 3.0·(w'')^(−1.85)`). Eq. 3 is eq. 2,
  `τ = 2.0×10⁵·(w''/H_c)^(−1.85)` with H_c the crib height in mm, at H_c = 610 mm, fitted to
  Walton's 305 and 610 mm crib tests (NISTIR 88-3696) "within ± 50 %". The fitted data run from
  0.026 to 0.133 mm/s, most of them above 0.07 mm/s, and the report warns that at low rates the
  power law over-predicts the time to extinguish; the module states that range. For a rate that changes, `dQ/dt = −Q/τ(w''(t))`. Growth stops
  while `w'' ≥ fire:criticalWaterRate`. Below it, water cools but the fire still grows. The
  critical rate is the fuel's (TODO(verify) published values for wood cribs: Tamanini, "The
  Application of Water Sprays to the Extinguishment of Crib Fires", *Combust. Sci. Technol.* 14,
  pp. 17–23, 1976, as NISTIR 5254 cites it, and Rasbash; neither text was openly readable).
- **Energy check.** Water that reaches the flame zone takes up `c_w·(100 °C − T_w) + h_fg` per kg,
  with `h_fg = 2.256 MJ/kg` at 100 °C (the latent heat of vaporisation; corrected from 2.257: the
  NIST Chemistry WebBook's saturated water table gives h_v − h_l = 2278.7 kJ/kg at 91.46 °C and
  2249.7 kJ/kg at 102.53 °C, 2256 kJ/kg at 100 °C between them; IAPWS-IF97 itself not opened). That is about 2.6 MJ/kg from 20 °C. The
  module reports `fire/<n>/water_cooling` (W, what the water absorbs) and steam mass. A test checks
  that the suppression never removes heat faster than the water could absorb it.
- **Class B.** Water on a burning liquid pool does not put it out in this model (it sinks under
  the fuel). It only cools surroundings. Foam does (§5.3). This follows the fire classes'
  guidance on extinguishing agents (TODO(verify) NFPA 10's wording: not openly readable; OSHA
  29 CFR 1910.155 defines the classes but not the agents for each).
- **Class C.** Water on energised equipment is allowed by the physics but flagged:
  `fire/<n>/hazard` is true, and a robot's ROS can read it.

#### 5.2 Wind and downwash

- **Tilt** (§3), which moves the radiation source and the smoke.
- **Blow-off.** A small diffusion flame goes out when the air speed at it exceeds a critical
  value that depends on the fuel and the flame's size. No general correlation was found for this
  draft that covers arbitrary fuel items, so it is a fuel parameter, `fire:blowOffSpeed`, given
  by the user from a measurement, and absent (never) by default. Large fires do not blow off;
  they lean and burn harder. Blow-off applies only while `Q` is below `fire:blowOffMaxHrr`
  (an optional attribute, open question 7).
- **Downwash.** The aerodynamics domain already writes each tether node's rotor wake
  (`tether/<n>/downwash`, rotor_aero.md §2.9). It writes the same at each fire's base,
  `fire/<n>/downwash` (m/s, world-local), which the fire adds to the wind. A drone that hovers low
  over a small flame can blow it out, and one over a large fire tilts and spreads it. Neither
  module calls the other (invariant 1).
- **Wind-driven burning-rate increase** of pool fires is not modelled (open question 8).

#### 5.3 Smothering

- **Foam or a blanket.** The foam applied (from a nozzle with `act:nozzle:agent = foam`) covers
  a fraction `φ_foam` of the burning area: the volume of finished foam laid down over the area,
  divided by a blanket thickness (TODO(verify) the minimum application rates and blanket
  thicknesses NFPA 11 gives, which become the preset values: NFPA 11 is not openly readable;
  Scheffey's SFPE chapter 47, "Foam Agents and AFFF System Design Considerations", is the other
  source). The burning area shrinks as
  `A(1 − φ_foam)`, and the fire goes out at `φ_foam = 1`. A fire blanket is the same with
  `φ_foam = 1` over its footprint.
- **Oxygen in an enclosure.** A `FireCompartmentAPI_1` prim (a volume, and openings with area and
  height) makes its fires share one well-mixed oxygen balance:

  ```
  d(m·Y_O2)/dt = ṁ_in·Y_O2,∞ − ṁ_out·Y_O2 − Q/ΔH_O2
  ṁ_in ≈ 0.5·A_o·√H_o  kg/s      (Kawagoe's ventilation limit for an opening of area A_o, height H_o)
  ```

  `ΔH_O2 ≈ 13.1 MJ per kg of oxygen` is Huggett's constant (C. Huggett, "Estimation of Rate of
  Heat Release by Means of Oxygen Consumption Measurements", *Fire and Materials* 4(2), pp. 61–65,
  1980; the FDS Technical Reference Guide 6.11.1, eq. 5.59: "approximately 13100 kJ/kg for most
  fuels"; NIST SP 958's summary page: "Huggett recommended the constant 13.1 kJ/g"). A fire goes
  out when the oxygen mole fraction falls below `fire:oxygenLimit` (the fuel's limiting oxygen
  concentration; Beyler, "Flammability Limits of Premixed and Diffusion Flames", SFPE Handbook
  5th ed., chapter 17; TODO(verify) values: the chapter is not openly readable). Closing a door (an opening's area is a signal) or flooding the
  room with CO2 (`X_O2 = 0.2095·(1 − X_CO2)`) puts it out at the time the balance gives. In open
  air there is no compartment, and oxygen never limits.
- **A CO2 extinguisher in open air** dilutes the air only locally and briefly. The model is a
  well-mixed parcel around the flame, fed by the nozzle's agent flow, with a residence time from
  the plume's entrainment. It is the least validated part (open question 9).

### 6. What sensors see

- **Thermal cameras** (`ThermalCameraAPI_1`): each pixel's ray is tested against each flame
  (a tilted cone of height L and base D; closed form). Through it the apparent radiance is
  `ε_f·B(T_f) + (1 − ε_f)·(what is behind)`, with `ε_f = 1 − exp(−κ·s)` over the path length s in
  the flame, `T_f = fire:flameTemperature`, and B the camera's band-integrated Planck radiance
  (`sensors::ThermalBand`, existing). Smoke attenuates by its scaled σ.
- **Visible cameras and the viewport**: the realistic renderer draws a flame as an emissive,
  animated volume in the same cone (a drawing choice for its look, with its size and tilt from
  the physics). It also adds the flame as a lamp (RFC 0021): a point source at mid-height with
  luminous intensity `χ_r·Q·K(T_f)/(4π)`, where `K(T_f)` is the luminous efficacy of a black body
  at the flame temperature (Planck weighted by the CIE photopic curve, computed once). Night fires
  light their surroundings, and auto exposure reacts.
- **Lidars**: flames are transparent (soot in a flame is not dense enough for a hard return in
  this model; open question 11). Smoke attenuates and clutters (§4).
- **Sensor kinds**: `visibility_sensor` reads smoke; the `thermistor` and `ir_thermometer` probe
  kinds (`lagrange_thermal_probe`) read the heated parts and the flame's apparent temperature
  through the same scene.

### 7. Spread

A fuel item with `fire:ignitionTemperature` and a `ThermalAPI_1` node ignites when its node
reaches that temperature. Its node is heated by the radiation of §3 from the burning items around
it, through the existing thermal network. This is the surface-temperature ignition criterion
(Babrauskas, *Ignition Handbook*, 2003; TODO(verify) the chapter: not openly readable; Torero,
"Flaming Ignition of Solid Fuels", SFPE Handbook 5th ed., chapter 21, is another source). It is crude for thick solids,
whose surface heats ahead of the lumped node, and the documentation says so. The scenario can
also ignite an item at `fire:ignitionTime`, by `fire/<n>/ignite` (Bool input), or from ROS (§11).

### 8. The nozzle (an actuator, RFC 0020)

`NozzleAPI_1` (in RFC 0020's `schemas/actuator`) on a link: `act:nozzle:diameter`,
`dischargeCoefficient`, `agent` (`water`, `foam`, `co2`), `foamExpansion`, `sprayHalfAngle`
(0: a solid stream), and a `tank` relation to a prim with `act:tank:capacity` and
`act:tank:initial`. A pump is a pressure source on the electrical bus
(`P = Δp·Q_v/η_pump`, an `ElectricalLoadAPI_1` of model `signal`).

- Exit velocity `v0 = C_d·√(2Δp/ρ)`, mass flow `ṁ = ρ·A·v0`. The jet's reaction on the robot is
  `F = ṁ·v0` against the jet's direction, on the nozzle's body as `body/<b>/wrench/nozzle`. This
  matters for a drone holding a hose.
- The jet's path is ballistic from the nozzle, and drag-free in phase 1. A real stream breaks up
  and falls short of that path, which the documentation states (TODO(verify) a published fire
  stream reach correlation to add: none found in an openly readable source in this pass). It is cut by the first collider it meets (scene rays along the
  path). Its landing footprint is a disc whose radius grows with `sprayHalfAngle` and distance.
  The nozzle writes `act/<z>/impact` (the point, m), `act/<z>/flow` (kg/s) and
  `act/<z>/footprint` (m). The fire module turns those into each fire's `w''` from the overlap of
  the footprint with the burning area.
- The water in the tank is carried as a weight, `−m_w·g` at the tank's position
  (`body/<b>/wrench/tank`), shrinking as it empties. The engines' body masses do not change at
  run time. The tank's inertia and sloshing are left out (open question 12).
- Signals in: `act/<z>/command` (0–1 valve), `act/<z>/pressure_command` (Pa). Out: as above, and
  `act/<z>/tank_mass` (kg). ROS: `/<z>/command` `std_msgs/Float64`, `/<z>/tank` `std_msgs/Float64`.

### 9. Drawing (invariant 14), with no protocol change

- Flames: fire prims arrive in the scene snapshot (`api_schemas` contains `FireAPI_1`); their
  `fire/<n>/hrr`, `flame_height`, `flame_tilt` and `flame_tilt_direction` come over the signal
  subscription. The viewer draws them with the renderer that draws them for cameras.
- Smoke: `env/smoke/extinction` is a field the server offers like `aero/wind_speed` and
  `thermal/cells`: slices (`FieldSlice`) and boxes (`FieldVolume` with `components = 1`, as
  `thermal/cells` already sends). The viewer draws the box as a volume in the Realistic shading
  and as a slice in the Environment workspace.
- The water jet: `Overlay.KIND_PATH` (exists since 1.0, unused), the jet's path points, drawn as
  a stream. UI work follows `docs/design/ui/` and `.claude/rules/viewer-ui.md`.

### 10. FDS offline, as OpenFOAM is for air

NIST's Fire Dynamics Simulator is the reference CFD code for fire. Its licence, read from
`github.com/firemodels/fds/LICENSE.md` on 2026-11-17, is a NIST notice, not GPL: the software is
"not subject to copyright protection within the United States", it may be used, copied, modified
and distributed provided the notice is kept intact, modifications are marked and NIST is
acknowledged, it is provided "AS IS", and it is not intended for uses where failure could cause
injury. Lagrange still keeps it as a separate program and exchanges files only, as with
OpenFOAM (invariant 12): one way to run it, nothing to link, no question of combining licences
(open question 2).

- `lagrange fds SCENE --fire NAME [--seconds N] [--cell M] [--out DIR] [--write-only]
  [--read-only] [--container]` writes an FDS input file: obstructions from the scene's collision
  boxes and hulls (voxelised to the FDS mesh), the fire as a burner with its HRR per area and a
  ramp from §2, the wind as boundary conditions, and slice and 3D outputs of soot density,
  temperature and velocity. It runs FDS (an installed one, or a container; TODO(verify) an
  official FDS container image and tag: the `firemodels` GitHub organisation has none, the
  6.11.1 release ships Linux, macOS and Windows installers only, and the User's Guide names no
  container), and converts the outputs. FDS's own converter, `fds2ascii`
  (User's Guide 6.11.1, §22.11), is interactive: it asks on standard input for the CHID, the file
  type (Plot3D, slice or boundary), a sampling factor, a box, an averaging time span and the
  variables, and writes one `CHID_fds2ascii.csv`. `lagrange fds` answers those prompts and turns
  the CSV into NanoVDB itself (corrected: fds2ascii does not write NanoVDB). It writes
  `smoke.nvdb` (extinction, K_m·soot density), `temperature.nvdb` and `wind.nvdb`. A run then uses
  them as fields: the smoke as a static or time-stepped extinction field, the wind through the
  existing `--weather wind_field=`.
- The same tool compares Lagrange's plume and radiation with FDS for a scene (temperatures and
  flux at points), as `lagrange_foam_compare` does for pressure.

## Substitutes, tiers, step

- Domain `fire`, step 100 ms (growth and suppression take seconds to minutes; puffs move at most
  a metre per step in a strong plume). Smoke field at 2 Hz.
- Substitutes: `zero` (no fires: `hrr` 0, no smoke, no heat), `constant` (every authored fire at
  its full-involvement HRR, unaffected by water, wind or oxygen: a steady fire for sensor tests),
  `replay` (`fire/*` and the smoke field from MCAP; the field is left out of recordings if over
  the array limit, as other large arrays are, and replay then has no smoke).
- Tiers: the model, the smoke puffs and the field, the radiation rays and the nozzle run on the
  CPU on every tier, deterministically. Drawing flames and smoke volumes needs a Vulkan device.
  On Tier 0 software rendering the flame is a simple emissive cone and the smoke a uniform veil
  along each pixel's ray (the existing fog path).

  Including Conduction Effects", *Fire Safety Journal* 14, pp. 113–125, 1988, as the FDS Technical

## Open questions (with a proposed answer)

1. *A built-in domain, or a plugin?* Built in, as tethers are: fire feeds thermal, sensors,
   renderers and the viewer, which plugins cannot reach (RFC 0021 §1).
2. *FDS as a separate program, files only, even though its licence would allow more?* Yes: one
   rule for offline solvers, nothing linked, and FDS is a Fortran/MPI build no package depends on.
3. *Ship fuel presets with numbers from the SFPE Handbook and Babrauskas?* Yes, as individual
   values with citations, after the owner confirms that is acceptable (facts with a source,
   not a copied table). Until then presets ship empty and samples author their own values with
   sources.
4. *χ_r fallback 0.3 or required?* Required. Radiative fractions range widely between fuels,
   and the 1.x rule is no device values in fallbacks. Presets give it.
5. *Burn-out decay over the last 20 % of fuel?* Yes, as a stated modelling choice, with a
   `fire:decayFraction` attribute (fallback 0.2) for users with a measured curve; or an HRR table
   (`fire:hrrCurve`, time and W pairs) that replaces §2 entirely. Proposed: both.
6. *Smoke by puffs, or by the live LBM air box (`aerodynamics::LbmBox`)?* Puffs first (any size,
   any tier, deterministic). Advecting smoke in the LBM box when it runs is a later step: it would
   give smoke round obstacles and in the rotors' wake for a small volume.
7. *Blow-off with no general correlation?* Keep it a user parameter, off by default, plus an
   optional `fire:blowOffMaxHrr`. Search the literature for a fuel-item correlation before step 3
   (TODO(verify): not searched in this pass), and add it as a preset value if one is found.
8. *Wind raising a pool fire's burning rate?* Not in phase 1. Documented.
9. *Local CO2 dilution in open air?* Ship it behind `fire.co2_open_air: true` (default false),
   documented as unvalidated, until a published extinguisher test with enough inputs is found.
10. *Dry powder and clean agents?* Not in this RFC.
11. *Lidar returns from flames?* None. Revisit if a published lidar-in-flame measurement is found.
12. *Tank sloshing and inertia?* Left out. The tank is a weight only. For a drone with a large
    tank, the docs say the inertia is understated.
13. *Pre-approve the gated merges (schemas) of steps 2–7 once each passes its tests?* Yes.
